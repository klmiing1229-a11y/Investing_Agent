# Research behind the W- rules

Web research gathered October 2026. Every `W-` rule in [`KB.md`](KB.md) comes from a source below.
`W-` rules rank BELOW your own `R-` rules. Where a paper was paywalled, the abstract or a named
secondary summary was used, and that is said. Check the originals before relying on a figure.

## 1. What the job is

- **Quant researcher vs quant trader.** Researchers find a statistical relationship, prove it is real out of sample, and turn it into a tradable signal. Their feedback loop is weeks to months. Traders own live risk and P&L, watch fills, costs and anomalies, and intervene when the model meets a situation it wasn't built for. Their P&L is daily. Firm type matters more than the title.
  Sources: [TraderMath](https://www.tradermath.org/articles/quant-trader-vs-quant-researcher) · [IMC](https://www.imc.com/us/articles/quant-research-vs-quant-trading-what-s-the-difference-at-imc) · [Optiver](https://www.optiver.com/join-us/stories/breaking-down-the-trading-industry-which-role-is-right-for-you/) · [techinterview.org day-in-life](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474729/day-in-life-quant-researcher-trader-developer-strats/)
- **Goldman "Strats".** Goldman's own postings: strats use maths and science training to create products, advise on transactions, measure risk and find market opportunities. Some sit on desks building pricing and empirical market-behaviour models; some build automated trading algorithms; some work with sales and clients. Postings ask for a quantitative degree plus Python/R/Matlab.
  Source: [Goldman Sachs careers (higher.gs.com)](https://higher.gs.com/roles/160563) · [GS Quantitative Engineering posting](https://jobs.anitab.org/companies/goldman-sachs/jobs/46071617-quantitative-engineering-analyst-global-banking-markets)
- **Skills.** Probability, statistics, linear algebra; Python (pandas, NumPy, statsmodels); market structure; backtesting; risk management. Interviews: QT = mental maths, fast probability, market-making games, sizing; QR = statistics/ML depth, regression pathologies.
  Sources: [QuantStart self-study plan](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Self-Study-Plan-for-Becoming-a-Quantitative-Trader-Part-I/) · [CFI: How to become a quant](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/learn/resources/career/how-to-become-a-quant)
- **Books** (named from general knowledge, not from a search result): Chan *Quantitative Trading*; Hull *Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives*; Natenberg *Option Volatility and Pricing*; López de Prado *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*; Hyndman & Athanasopoulos *Forecasting: Principles and Practice* (free online, recommended by QuantStart).

## 2. Signals with published evidence

- **Cross-sectional momentum** — Jegadeesh & Titman (1993), *Journal of Finance* 48(1):65–91. Buying past 3–12-month winners and selling losers earned significant positive returns over 3–12-month holding periods; part of the gain dissipates in the next two years. Abstract via [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v48y1993i1p65-91.html), summary via [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/recognizing-an-investing-signal-that-defied-wisdom-and-endured/).
- **Short-term reversal** — Jegadeesh (1990) and Lehmann (1990): last-month (and last-week) winners tend to reverse. This is why academics measure momentum as "12-1" (skip the most recent month). Secondary: [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-short-term-return-reversal/), [Columbia working paper](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/11568/str1.pdf).
- **Time-series momentum** — Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), *Journal of Financial Economics* 104:228–250. Each of 58 liquid futures showed significant TSMOM: an asset's own past 12-month return predicts its next 1–12 months, with partial reversal at longer horizons. Critique: Kim, Tse & Wald attribute the result to volatility scaling, and it weakened in 2009–2013. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463) · [Yale-hosted PDF](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/jpde.pdf) · [Quantpedia critique summary](https://quantpedia.com/deconstructing-the-time-series-momentum-strategy/)
- **Momentum crashes** — Daniel & Moskowitz (2016), *JFE* 122(2):221–247. Momentum crashes happen in "panic" states (after market declines, with high volatility) during sharp rebounds; past losers' beta can exceed 3. Partly forecastable; long-only momentum crashes less. [NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)
- **Trend filter / moving averages** — Faber (2007), *Journal of Wealth Management*: a 10-month SMA timing rule reduced volatility and drawdowns across asset classes (secondary summaries; out-of-sample JSE test in a [Univ. of Pretoria thesis](https://repository.up.ac.za/items/7f27d00d-4c0e-4826-acf0-1a7db806d25d)). Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992), *JF* 47(5): MA and trading-range-break rules on the Dow 1897–1986; returns after buy signals were higher and less volatile. BUT Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999) showed data-snooping weakens it, and later studies found the edge faded after 1986. [RePEc BLL](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html) · [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/old-school-academics-on-moving-average-rules-remarkable/)
- **Value and momentum together** — Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013), *JF* 68(3):929–985. Value and momentum premia appear in eight markets and asset classes and are negatively correlated, so combining them beats either alone. [NYU PDF](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf)
- **Low beta** — Frazzini & Pedersen (2014), "Betting Against Beta", *JFE*. Long low-beta/short high-beta earned positive risk-adjusted returns in US and 19 international markets; explanation: leverage-constrained investors bid up high-beta assets. [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/betting-against-beta-bab-construction/)
- **Crypto** — Liu & Tsyvinski (2021), *RFS* 34(6):2689–2727. Crypto returns load on crypto-specific network/adoption factors, have little exposure to stock and macro factors, and show strong time-series momentum; investor-attention proxies forecast returns. [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v34y2021i6p2689-2727..html) · [NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)

## 3. Risk and sizing

- **Volatility-managed portfolios** — Moreira & Muir (2017), *JF* 72(4):1611–1644. Variance is forecastable at short horizons while returns barely are, so cutting exposure when recent volatility is high raised Sharpe ratios (market: ~25% higher). [NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/22208)
- **Kelly criterion** — maximises long-run growth but suggests very large bets; errors in estimated MEANS matter far more than errors in variances (Chopra & Ziemba 1993, cited in MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba). Practitioners use fractional Kelly (¼–½). Half-Kelly figures (~75% of growth, ~25% of variance) are from secondary sources and unverified. [MacLean–Thorp–Ziemba "Good and bad properties of Kelly"](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf) · [Wikipedia: Kelly criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion)
- **Sharpe ratio statistics** — Lo (2002), *Financial Analysts Journal* 58(4). Scaling a monthly Sharpe by √12 is only right for serially uncorrelated returns; with positive autocorrelation the annualised Sharpe can be overstated by more than 65%. [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2002/the-statistics-of-sharpe-ratios)
- **Trading costs** — Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016), *RFS* 29(1):104–147. Costs lower the profitability and significance of every anomaly; most strategies with over 50% monthly one-sided turnover fail net of costs; the best fix is a buy/hold spread (a stricter bar to open a position than to keep it). [NBER w20721](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)

## 4. Evidence standards

- **Multiple testing** — Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), *RFS* 29(1):5–68: because hundreds of factors have been tried, a new factor needs a t-statistic above 3.0, not the usual 2.0. [NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)
- **Backtest overfitting** — Bailey & López de Prado (2014) Deflated Sharpe Ratio: adjust a backtest's Sharpe for the number of trials and for non-normal returns. Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu: Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation; plain hold-out tests are unreliable for backtests. [DSR, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551) · [PBO, SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)
- **Biases to name** (general knowledge): look-ahead bias (using data you couldn't have had at the time), survivorship bias (testing only on assets that still exist), data snooping (trying many rules and reporting the winner).

## What this does NOT give the agent

- No backtest of its own rules exists. Nothing above proves R-001…R-012 work on your tickers.
- Most results are long-short, multi-asset portfolios. A retail investor holding a few names long-only is a different situation. A factor premium is a statement about thousands of positions, not about one share of TSLA.
